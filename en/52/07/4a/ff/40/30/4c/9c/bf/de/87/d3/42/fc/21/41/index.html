English from Scratch: Start Here

Series by @carlos_djc

Hi, I'm Carlos. If you've ever said "I understand a bit of English, but I can't speak it", this series is for you. It happened to me too: I knew the rules by heart, and when someone greeted me in English, my mind went blank.

In English from Scratch we'll learn the way people really learn: with phrases you'll actually use, saying them out loud, a little every day. This is the first lesson: how we'll work and your first conversation.

How we'll learn

  • Phrases before rules. First you learn to say something useful; grammar comes later, to understand why it's said that way.
  • One topic per lesson. Short lessons you can finish in a little while.
  • Pronunciation from day one. Every phrase comes with its pronunciation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), the one dictionaries use.
  • A little every day. Fifteen minutes a day are worth more than three hours on Sunday.

Your first conversation

With these eight phrases you can already greet someone, introduce yourself and ask where someone is from. Read them out loud:

PhrasePronunciationWhen to use it
Hi! I'm Carlos./haɪ, aɪm ˈkɑːrloʊs/To greet and introduce yourself
What's your name?/wʌts jɔːr neɪm/To ask someone's name
My name is Ana./maɪ neɪm ɪz ˈɑːnə/To say your name
Nice to meet you./naɪs tə miːt juː/When you meet someone for the first time
Where are you from?/wer ɑːr juː frʌm/To ask where someone is from
I'm from Peru./aɪm frʌm pəˈruː/To say where you are from
How are you?/haʊ ɑːr juː/To ask how someone is
I'm fine, thanks. And you?/aɪm faɪn, θæŋks. ænd juː/To answer and ask back

A pronunciation tip: the th in thanks (/θ/) doesn't exist in many languages. Put the tip of your tongue between your teeth and blow gently. It feels strange at first; that's normal.

Common mistakes when starting out

Most of these mistakes come from translating word by word from your own language. They're especially common if your first language is Spanish. If you've already made them, don't worry: we've all been there.

  • You don't "have" an age, you "are" it. Don't say I have 20 years. Say I'm 20 or I'm 20 years old.
  • "Agree" is a verb. It's not I am agree, but I agree.
  • People is plural. It's not people is, but people are.
  • False friends: actually means "in fact", not "currently"; and embarrassed means "ashamed", not "pregnant" (a classic for Spanish speakers, who hear embarazada).

Your 15-minute daily plan

  1. 5 minutes listening: a short video, a song or a podcast in English. It doesn't matter if you don't understand everything.
  2. 5 minutes repeating out loud: the phrases from the lesson, copying the rhythm. If you can, record yourself and listen back.
  3. 5 minutes writing: three sentences about yourself with what you learned. Today, for example: your name, where you're from and how you are.
